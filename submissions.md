The Economic Historian welcomes original research, historiographical essays, quantitative and archival work, as well as essays that adapt existing scholarship for a broader audience. Preference will be given to original contributions, although previously published research may form the basis of a submission. Essays making use of maps and other visuals are especially encouraged.

## Style
The Economic Historian strives to be a journal of ideas not an academic journal. With this, writing is nevertheless expected to be prepared with an educated and discerning audience in mind. Lively, thoughtful, and intelligent prose is highly valued. Essays should be written for a general reader rather than a specialist audience, with the argument clear from the opening pages. We do not publish papers in the conventional academic format, with a literature review and a statement of contribution.

## Length

Word length should be guided by intellectual necessity; most published work runs between 4,000 and 12,000 words. 

## References

Use endnotes for citations and for brief comments that would interrupt the text. Charts and tables may be included where they support the argument; please send the underlying data with them.
